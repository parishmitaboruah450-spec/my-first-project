Your ice cream was never fully frozen.
It's four things: ice crystals, tiny air bubbles, fat, and a sugary syrup.
Sugar stops it freezing at zero. Ice cream only starts freezing around minus two and a half degrees.
Even in your freezer, some syrup stays liquid. That's why you can scoop it.
Warm it up, and the ice crystals turn back into water.
Here's the twist: melting ice is only half the story. What drips is a foam collapsing.
Clumps of fat form a net around the air bubbles. A strong net holds its shape. A weak one slumps into a puddle.
That's why makers add stabilizers. They keep ice crystals small, so it melts slower.
